how it works

Capture. Prove. Share.

1 · Capture

QorCap is the one app you run, on Windows, during the match. It records the capture card and fingerprints the video as it is written: every segment is hashed and chained, with timestamped checkpoints. It stays light, so it doesn't strain your laptop.

controller button log in the same session · since v0.3.0

2 · Prove

After the final whistle, QorTime works on the finished recording. It re-checks the fingerprints, reads the score, and seals a signed receipt. The AI referee reviews the evidence and signs a verdict: accept, dispute, or abstain, with plain reasons and the evidence frames.

3 · Share

Send the receipt to your opponent, a tournament, or a platform. Anyone can check it in the free browser verifier, and nothing is uploaded. A hosted referee countersign, Verified Match, is in test-mode early access.

Inside the Prove step

  • Integrity checks in code: video fingerprints, checkpoint chain, and signatures. Scores can only move in legal football steps, never backwards. The clock only runs down within a quarter. The final screen must match the last score.
  • AI referee: reads frames to confirm the score. It only accepts a reading when it is confident and the reading agrees across several frames, and it abstains rather than guessing. Tampering is caught by the integrity checks, not by trusting a model.
  • Honest limit: QorTime proves the footage's integrity and timing (and, since QorCap v0.3.0, the controller inputs from the same session). It does not prove who held the controller.

02 / challenge

Prove a human is on the sticks.

Three referee challenges per match: game start, the second half, and the fourth quarter. The player pauses and enters the pattern. On QorCap rule v2 that pause has to show up within 2.5 seconds. A relay under 2.5 seconds is not reliably caught.

QorCap logs the controller since v0.3.0. Since v0.3.11 a challenge only opens when Madden lets you pause: after the kickoff and a play call, never during a kickoff. Receipts with no rule id still use the older 600 ms ceiling.

03 / co-sign

The laptop signs the chain. The referee timestamps it.

Checkpoint countersignatures come from the laptop’s own key. The official referee does not treat those as live: the key sits on the player’s PC. What the official referee does sign is the chain head, about every 60 seconds, plus the three challenges. A receipt forged later cannot invent those timestamps.

04 / seal

The receipt prints. Everyone signs.

At the final whistle the player’s key signs the result. A receipt from one console is the one-player tier. Two-sided corroboration is a separate indicator. It never changes the verdict. It is available from QorCap v0.3.31 when both players pair their match: each client sends the referee’s pairing token and a signed score timeline. It has not yet been tested on a real two-player match.

05 / verify

One verdict. In your browser.

Paste the JSON into the verifier. It checks every signature, the checkpoint chain and the challenges locally and returns a verdict. Nothing is uploaded.

Honest limits

QorTime proves the footage's integrity and timing. Controller inputs from the same session are logged by QorCap since v0.3.0. It does not prove:

  • Account sharing: who is physically holding the controller. The referee does not check pad identity. QorCap’s second-pad note, when it exists, stays in the local input_proof.json.
  • Collusion: both players agreeing to throw or fake a match.
  • Anything outside what the capture card saw.
  • Red team: a virtual capture device was missed. Relays under about 2.5–3 seconds, and a splice that is rehashed and resigned, came out weak. QorCap red-team notes.
